# Autumn Marketing Dashboard — Data Research & Modeling Specification

**Purpose.** This document is the single source of truth for the synthetic dataset behind the
Autumn marketing dashboard demo. It identifies a **real, independently operated US hotel**,
records what is **publicly verifiable** about it, gathers **hospitality industry benchmarks**,
and specifies a **766-day modeled marketing program** that a deterministic Supabase seed
generator can implement.

**What is real and what is not.**

| Layer | Status |
|---|---|
| Property identity, room count, room names, ownership, location, booking engine, historic status | **REAL** — publicly documented, cited below |
| Destination visitation, visitor-origin split, festivals, seasonality | **REAL** — public DMO / press / tourism sources |
| Conversion rates, CTRs, CPCs, ADR curves, direct-booking share, ROAS | **INDUSTRY BENCHMARK** — published third-party research, not this hotel's |
| Every booking, impression, click, visit, dollar of revenue in the database | **SYNTHETIC** — generated by the model in this document |

> **No private hotel data is claimed.** Historic Hotel Nichols' actual bookings, revenue, ADR,
> occupancy, ad spend, analytics and guest origins are not public and are not in this dataset.
> The property supplies a realistic *shape* — size, seasonality, rate band, market — around
> which a plausible marketing program is modeled.

Classification tags used throughout:

- `VERIFIED-PROPERTY` — publicly documented fact about the specific hotel
- `VERIFIED-MARKET` — publicly documented fact about the destination
- `BENCHMARK` — published hospitality/industry research, not property-specific
- `MODELED` — reasoned assumption constrained by the layers above
- `UNKNOWN` — not publicly available; deliberately not invented

## 2. Selected primary hotel

### **Historic Hotel Nichols — 201 Center Street, South Haven, Michigan**

**Why it wins:**

1. **Best-documented room inventory of any candidate.** Wikipedia states 17 rooms and 2 suites;
   the hotel's own Rooms & Suites page independently lists exactly 19 named units. Two
   independent sources agree — rare at this property size.
2. **Verifiable independence and long family operation.** Established 1884 and
   family-operated for more than a century, through a documented succession of
   owning families ending with the current owners (since 2017). Independently
   operated throughout, with no chain affiliation. It has *not* been the same
   family since 1884 — ownership changed hands in 1944, 2002 and 2017.
3. **National Register of Historic Places listing (April 2025).** Gives the property a real,
   citable identity and a genuine brand-search story — exactly the asset Autumn's
   "Brand Protection" campaign type exists to defend.
4. **Identifiable booking stack.** Room pages link to `hotelnichols.client.innroad.com`, so the
   direct-booking channel is real and the booking engine (innRoad) is publicly identifiable.
   This makes "direct bookings attributed to Autumn" a coherent concept rather than a fiction.
5. **Severe, real seasonality.** A downtown Lake Michigan beach-town hotel with a documented
   February/March rate trough and an August peak anchored on a 50,000-attendee festival.
6. **A real, non-obvious feeder-market story.** Visit South Haven's Placer.ai analysis found
   the majority of visitors are Michiganders (52%), not Chicagoans (Illinois 18%) — while
   overall visitation is *flat to declining* and rival towns gain. This gives the dashboard a
   genuine "one market growing, one market declining" narrative that is grounded in published
   destination research rather than invented.
7. **Correct customer size for Autumn.** 19 keys is squarely an owner-operator business where a
   marketing platform's job is to prove incremental direct revenue.

**Accepted trade-off:** at 19 keys the absolute revenue numbers are small (~$800K modeled annual
room revenue). That is the honest consequence of choosing a real property over a convenient one.
The dashboard is designed for exactly this operator, so small-but-real is the right calibration.

**Secondary properties** (for a multi-tenant schema demo):

- **Yelton Manor Boutique Hotel B&B**, South Haven — 17 rooms, adults-only, higher-touch B&B
  positioning. Different guest profile from Nichols at nearly identical size.
- **Waters View Inn**, South Haven — 14 accommodations including a cottage and condo-style units,
  family- and pet-friendly, wide published rate range ($208–$1,103). Different mix logic.

## 5. Feeder-market evidence

The strongest real finding in the research, and it **overturns the obvious assumption** — but it
describes the destination, not this hotel.

| Origin | Share of South Haven **destination visitors** | Class |
|---|---|---|
| **Michigan** | **52%** | `VERIFIED-MARKET` (Placer.ai via Visit South Haven, 2025) |
| **Illinois** | **18%** | `VERIFIED-MARKET` |
| All other states | ~30% | Derived from the two verified figures |

Reported explicitly as a correction to local belief: *the majority of South Haven's visitors are
from Michigan, not Chicago.*

### How this is used — and how it is not

These are **destination-level priors**, not Hotel Nichols' booking mix. Two limits matter:

1. **Placer.ai counts visitors, not overnight hotel guests.** A location-data visitor count
   includes day-trippers, second-home owners and locals crossing a geofence. In-state share is
   therefore structurally overstated relative to a paid hotel's guest list — a family driving up
   from Kalamazoo for the afternoon is a visitor but never a room night.
2. **Overnight boutique-hotel guests travel farther than day visitors.** A two-hour Chicago drive
   is a reason to book a room; a forty-minute Holland drive often is not.

So the modeled mix in §8.9 **keeps Michigan collectively dominant** but sets it below the 52%
destination figure, and puts Illinois somewhat above 18% — while still landing Chicago far below
the 30% "it's all Chicago" assumption the previous dataset used.

| | Destination visitors (verified) | Modeled **hotel booking** mix |
|---|---|---|
| Michigan | 52% | **44%** — still the largest bloc |
| Illinois | 18% | **22%** |
| Everywhere else | ~30% | **34%** |

**City-level breakdown is `UNKNOWN`** — Placer.ai DMA detail was not published. Every individual
city weight in §8.9 is `MODELED`, ordered by drive time from South Haven:

- Kalamazoo ~45 min, Holland ~40 min, Grand Rapids ~1 hr, South Bend ~1.5 hr, Chicago ~2 hr,
  Detroit ~2.5 hr, Indianapolis ~4 hr.

### Destination trend — a demand constraint, not a market verdict

South Haven's visitation is documented as flat (2025 matched 2020) while New Buffalo and Holland
gained. That is applied in exactly one place: **`DESTINATION_TREND`, a small yearly multiplier on
hotel demand** (2024 1.01 / 2025 1.00 / 2026 0.985).

It is **not** treated as evidence that any particular feeder city declined. No published source
attributes the shift at metro level. Every city-level growth and decline factor in §8.9 is
therefore `MODELED` and labeled as such — including the Chicago softening, which is a modeling
choice informed by New Buffalo's documented gains, not a sourced fact about Chicago demand for
this hotel.

## 8. 766-day modeling assumptions

**Window: 2024-08-01 → 2026-09-05 inclusive = 766 days.** Contains two complete comparable
Aug–Jul years plus a partial current period, and ends at today's date so the dashboard's default
"last 30 days vs same period last year" has real data on both sides.

### 8.3 Monthly ADR — `MONTH_ADR`, index 0 = January

**This is a MODELED achieved rate, not Hotel Nichols' ADR.** The property's actual average daily
rate is private and is not claimed anywhere. What is public is a set of *advertised price
anchors* scraped by metasearch aggregators — a ~$222 average nightly price, a $145 floor, a
February/March trough, a $157–$467 recent booked band. The curve below is fitted to sit inside
those public anchors; what it produces is an assumed rate the model *achieves*, which is a
different thing from a rate the hotel *reports*.

```
[148, 145, 152, 172, 205, 262, 305, 312, 238, 198, 158, 168]
```

| Parameter | Baseline | Plausible range | Class | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Annual revenue-weighted ADR | **$228.6** | $205–$250 | `MODELED`, anchored on public prices | MEDIUM |
| February/March trough | $145–$152 | $135–$165 | `MODELED`, anchored on public prices | HIGH |
| July/August peak | $305–$312 | $280–$350 | `MODELED`, anchored on public prices | MEDIUM |
| December uplift over November | $168 vs $158 | — | `MODELED` | LOW |

*Rationale and check:* the curve is fitted so the occupancy-weighted mean equals **$228.6**,
against the publicly observed metasearch average of **$222** — a 3% gap, within noise. The
trough months match the publicly observed "cheapest in February and March". The peak sits inside
the observed $157–$467 recent booked band and inside the west-lakeshore $200–$350 benchmark.

Realised ADR in the generated data runs slightly above this curve (~$258/night across attributed
stays) because attributed guests skew toward weekends, peak months and the two suites. That is an
output of the model, not an input, and it is visible in the seed log.

### 8.4 Weekday effect

| Parameter | Value (index 0 = Sunday) | Class | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `WEEKDAY_DEMAND` | `[0.96, 0.74, 0.72, 0.78, 0.90, 1.38, 1.52]` | `MODELED` | MEDIUM |
| `WEEKDAY_ADR_FACTOR` | `[0.97, 0.88, 0.87, 0.90, 0.96, 1.16, 1.22]` | `MODELED` | MEDIUM |

*Rationale:* the only verified weekday signal is that **Monday is the cheapest night (~$174 vs
$222 average)** — a −22% Monday rate index. The curve is built so Monday lands at 0.88 × the
monthly ADR, and weekend uplift is set to the mirrored magnitude. Weekend-led demand is
characteristic of a documented drive-market leisure destination.

Normalization: multipliers are scaled so the weekday-weighted mean = 1.0 and monthly occupancy
targets are preserved.

## 10. Booking-event modeling assumptions

Generation order — **hotel economics first, marketing second**:

```
rooms (19)
  → available room nights          = 19 × days
  → seasonal demand                = MONTH_OCCUPANCY × WEEKDAY_DEMAND × events × noise
  → occupancy                      = min(demand, 1.0)            [hard cap]
  → ADR                            = MONTH_ADR × WEEKDAY_ADR_FACTOR × event × noise
  → sold room nights               = round(19 × occupancy)       [assert ≤ 19/day]
  → total modeled room revenue     = sold room nights × ADR
  → direct revenue                 = × directBookingShare (0.45)
  → Autumn-attributed revenue      = × autumnAttributedShareOfDirect (0.33/0.40/0.45)
  → booking events                 = attributed revenue ÷ (ADR × LOS), integerized
  → required website visits        = bookings ÷ campaign bookingConversion
  → ad clicks                      = visits ÷ clickToVisit
  → ad impressions                 = clicks ÷ ctr
  → ad spend                       = clicks × cpc × seasonalCpcMultiplier
```

Marketing volume is therefore **derived from** hotel capacity, never the reverse. It is
arithmetically impossible for this model to emit more bookings than the building can hold.

### Per-booking fields

| Field | Rule | Class |
|---|---|---|
| `check_in` | The stay night the demand model produced | derived |
| `room_nights` | Sampled from `NIGHT_WEIGHTS` / `PEAK_NIGHT_WEIGHTS` | `MODELED` |
| `check_out` | `check_in + room_nights` | derived |
| `booked_at` | `check_in − leadTime`, lognormal by season (§8.6) | `BENCHMARK_DERIVED` |
| `booking_value` | `Σ nightly ADR over the stay × campaign.adrFactor` — summed per night, so a stay spanning a rate change is priced correctly | derived |
| `campaign_id` | Weighted by each campaign's modeled booking production for that day | derived |
| `market_id` | Sampled from `MARKETS`, tilted by `campaign.marketTilt` and by 2026 growth factors | `MODELED` |
| `device` | Sampled from visit-share × conversion-index (§8.8) | `BENCHMARK_DERIVED` |
| `attributed_to_autumn` | `true` for generated rows; non-attributed direct volume is stored as aggregate context only | `MODELED` |
| `is_cancelled` | 11% of bookings, matching the 10.6–12.0% direct-cancellation benchmark | `BENCHMARK` |

### Determinism

| Requirement | Implementation |
|---|---|
| Reproducible | Seeded PRNG (e.g. mulberry32 / xoshiro) keyed on a constant `SEED` |
| Stable per-row | Derive each row's stream from `hash(SEED, date, campaignKey, index)` so adding a campaign does not reshuffle unrelated rows |
| No wall-clock, no `Math.random()` | Enforced by review; the same inputs must produce a byte-identical dataset every run |

years) and the **room ledger** runs 886 days (~2.43 years) because stays booked near the end of

### How the constraint is enforced in code

`supabase/generate.ts` routes every stay through a `RoomLedger` that refuses a claim if any night
of the stay already holds 19 rooms. An attributed booking that cannot fit shifts its arrival up to
six days and is otherwise dropped. `supabase/seed.ts` then refuses to write the dataset at all if
the peak night exceeds the room count, if sold room nights exceed available inventory, or if
attributed revenue exceeds total modeled revenue. `supabase/verify.ts` re-derives per-night
occupancy from the rows actually in Postgres and fails the run on any violation.

**Result: all 16 checks pass.** The one flagged item is ROAS at 6.05×, sitting just at the top of the
3–6× benchmark band.

## 12. Runtime rule — non-negotiable

The values in this document live **only** in `supabase/seed-model.ts`. They are inputs to the
generator that writes rows into Postgres.

- The seed script imports them. **The application must not.**
- Every number the dashboard renders must be a `SELECT` or an aggregate over seeded rows.
- No component may import `seed-model.ts`, and no constant here may be hard-coded into UI.
